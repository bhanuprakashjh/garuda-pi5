01 · Executive summary

One SDK, three hosts, and the only open autonomy stack in its power class

Garuda SDK is the software that turns Microchip's mission computer into an autonomy engine — and turns the Hailo acquisition into a platform rather than a part.

  • What it is. Twelve Linux services — synchronised camera capture, stereo visual-inertial odometry, mapping, planning, NPU inference, the PX4 bridge, time sync, health, logging, portal and OTA — running on the mission computer and speaking to a PX4 flight controller on PIC32CZ CA90 over Ethernet with PTP.
  • Why it wins. Every incumbent keeps its VIO closed: ModalAI ships Qualcomm binaries it cannot read; NVIDIA's cuVSLAM is a sealed GEM. Garuda's reference stack is permissively licensed end to end — customers extend it, audit it, and ship closed products on it. The accelerated kernels (v2, Hailo DSP) remain the retained moat.
  • How it de-risks. Development starts on Raspberry Pi 5 + Hailo-10H (A76 cores remove the CPU question; $130 accelerator; everything host-agnostic), then ports to the Hailo-15H SoM as a BSP exercise with measured per-stage budgets. The programme's biggest unknown becomes a number before the hard money is spent.
  • What it costs. Under $2,500 of external hardware, two core engineers plus part-time bench with AI-assisted development carrying ~75% of the software authoring, first GPS-denied flight targeted at week 16, usable v1 at month 9.
  • What is genuinely new. Three pieces: SLAM front-end kernels on the Hailo vector DSP (white space; toolchain access arrives with the acquisition), the PTP-disciplined trigger/strobe camera pipeline as a product, and AI-ISP tuning for night VIO. Everything else is disciplined integration of mature permissive open source — which is the point, not a weakness.
The one-sentence position

ModalAI sells a closed stack on one chip; NVIDIA sells a closed stack on a 15–60 W module; Garuda is the open stack on silicon we own end to end — flight control, propulsion, power, perception, networking and time — and the only one that keeps navigating when the satellites go away.

02 · What Garuda is

The companion half of a two-brain aircraft

The platform architecture is deliberately split: hard real-time flight control lives on the PIC32CZ CA90 — its own silicon, RTOS, IMUs and power rail — while everything computationally heavy lives on the mission computer under Linux. Garuda is the mission-computer half. Nothing in the inner control loop ever crosses Ethernet; odometry, obstacle data and mission goals do. If any Garuda service dies, the aircraft is still flown by the flight controller — that separation is a sales argument, an engineering principle and a certification story at once.

Design rules the whole SDK obeys

1. Failsafe gates on odometry validity and covariance — never on link liveness. Loss of the mission computer is a degraded mode, not a fallout. 2. One PTP clock domain: camera triggers are generated from the disciplined clock and the strobe returns to a capture input, so the EKF's external-vision delay is measured, not guessed. 3. The VIO IMU comes from the flight controller over DDS — timestamped at source in the shared timebase. 4. Every sensor and host sits behind a plugin interface, so tiers and cameras are swappable without touching the estimator.

06 · The timing chain

Why the clock is the product

Every VIO stack fights the same enemy: unknown latency between what the camera saw and what the IMU felt. Garuda's answer is structural — one disciplined clock owns the trigger, the strobe, the IMU and the estimator.

Fig. 6 — The trigger/strobe loop turns EKF2's delay parameter from a tuning exercise into a one-time measurement

Why competitors cannot copy this cheaply

The chain only closes because the switch, the PHYs, the oscillators and the holdover clocks are catalogue parts of the same vendor building the SDK. ModalAI cannot buy an atomic-holdover story from Qualcomm at any price; a DIY integrator can assemble the pieces but inherits the qualification burden Garuda ships as a done deliverable.

Reading it honestly

Where they win

Maturity, everywhere — VOXL has six years of flight hours and Blue UAS; Isaac has the research mindshare and raw compute; Auterion has enterprise fleet tooling. Garuda's answer is not to claim maturity it lacks but to publish benchmarks, logs and losing rows until the evidence accumulates.

Where Garuda wins now

Openness of the core, licence cleanliness, power class, silicon cost, fault isolation, night capability, the timing chain, and one-vendor scope. Five of those eight are structural — they follow from owning the silicon — and cannot be answered by a software release from any competitor.

The strategic difference

Every rival monetises the stack or the module. Garuda is free and open because it exists to sell fifteen other Microchip parts on the same aircraft. That inverts the incentive: the more customers fork it, the more silicon ships — which is why openness is the strategy, not a concession.

The AI-assisted working rules

Artifacts before code — real uname -a, package versions, camera SKUs and CA90 driver state are fed in before configs are generated. Repo, not fragments — output lands as reviewable commits with tests. Numbers are sacred — no benchmark is ever invented; gates come off the bench into datasets/baselines/. Structural review at midpoints. Red-team each phase — every phase closes with an explicit "argue this result is wrong" pass. Net effect measured on prior ports: ~75% of software authored by AI, calendar compressed by smaller team rather than shorter tail — the bench debug time remains human and irreducible.

Team: 2 core engineers + part-time hardware/bench through Phase 7; +1 for productisation; the same pair executes the Hailo-15 port. Humans own: all wiring and scope work, every empirical number, flight ops, ISP tuning judgement, architecture sign-off, and the v2 Cadence-toolchain kernels.

The closing frame

ModalAI needed six years because they also built the real-time partition, the IO layer and the VIO from Qualcomm's closed parts. Garuda inherits the NPU runtime with the acquisition, already flies the real-time half on our own silicon, and assembles the middle from mature permissive open source. Same destination — a fraction of the distance, and the only stack in the class that arrives open.
